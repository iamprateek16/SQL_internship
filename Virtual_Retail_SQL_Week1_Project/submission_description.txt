This report presents a comprehensive relational database model and architecture plan for a virtual retail environment, prepared as part of the Week 1 SQL Developer Internship task. The project begins with requirement analysis for customer relationship management, product catalog administration, supplier sourcing, warehouse inventory, sales and order tracking, payment processing, shipment fulfilment, product returns, and customer reviews. These business requirements are translated into a normalized MySQL 8.0 schema with clearly defined entities, primary keys, foreign keys, unique constraints, check constraints, and relationship cardinalities.

The data model includes customers and their addresses, products and categories, suppliers and product-supplier mappings, warehouses and inventory, retail orders and order items, payments, shipments, returns, return items, and reviews. The ER diagram illustrates the major relationships, while the architecture diagram explains the separation between presentation, application/business logic, data access, database persistence, and reporting. The report discusses First, Second, and Third Normal Form, explains historical order-price snapshots, and describes indexing decisions for common lookups and reporting queries.

The accompanying MySQL scripts create the database, insert sample data, and demonstrate practical SQL queries such as low-stock alerts, best-selling products, monthly revenue, customer order history, supplier catalog reporting, and order summaries. Scalability, concurrency, security, reliability, and future enhancements are also considered. This submission demonstrates how a structured relational design can support retail workflows while maintaining consistency, reducing duplication, and enabling useful business decision-making through SQL.
